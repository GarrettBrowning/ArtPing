Fight AI art theft

Your art is yours. Here is what ArtPing does to keep AI companies from training on it, and what you can do too.

What ArtPing does

  • Blocks AI training crawlers. Bots that collect images for AI training (like OpenAI's GPTBot, Anthropic's ClaudeBot, Common Crawl and ByteDance's Bytespider) are told to stay out, and turned away if they visit anyway.
  • Marks every image "do not train". Each image is sent with "noai" signals that AI tools are asked to respect.
  • Strips hidden data. Location (GPS) and other hidden details in your files are removed when you upload.

No website can fully stop a scraper that pretends to be a normal visitor. That's why we also recommend protecting the image itself, before you upload it.

Protect your art before uploading

Glaze protects your style: it makes small changes that are hard to see but stop AI from copying how you create. Nightshade goes further and "poisons" your image for any AI trained on it without permission. Both are free, made by the University of Chicago.

How to use Glaze
  1. Download Glaze for free from glaze.cs.uchicago.edu (Windows, or Mac with macOS 13 or newer). It needs about 5 GB of memory; a graphics card makes it faster but isn't required.
  2. No suitable computer? Ask for a free WebGlaze invite (it runs in your browser) by messaging @TheGlazeProject on Instagram or X.
  3. Finish your image first. Resize, crop and add your signature or watermark before glazing. ArtPing's limit is 10 MB, so size it down now if needed.
  4. Open Glaze and select your image. Set Intensity (higher = stronger protection, but more visible changes) and Render quality (higher = better protection, but slower; the top setting can take up to an hour per image).
  5. Click Preview to check the look, then Run. The glazed copy is saved to your output folder with the same file name. Keep your unglazed original somewhere safe and private.
  6. Upload the glazed copy here. A PNG keeps the protection best. Never glaze the same image twice; to make it stronger, glaze your original again with higher settings.
How to use Nightshade (optional)
  1. Download Nightshade for free from nightshade.cs.uchicago.edu. It works much like Glaze: finish your image first, then select it, pick an Intensity and Render quality, and run it.
  2. Important: Nightshade on its own does not protect your style. Its makers advise against posting images with only Nightshade if you're worried about style copying. To use both, run Nightshade first, then Glaze on the result (see their user guide).
  3. Its changes are easier to see on flat colors and smooth backgrounds. A low intensity setting is available if that matters for your piece.

Glaze and Nightshade are made by the Glaze Project at the University of Chicago; ArtPing isn't connected to them. They make AI training on your work much harder, but no tool is permanent protection.
